Big news · We made a game

Rites of Accord is live on Steam.

We developed Rites of Accord — a multiplayer, turn‑based, hex tactics game set on a future Earth where techno‑tribes resolve their conflicts through ceremonial digital battles instead of warfare.

For an indie game like ours, wishlists are everything: Steam’s algorithm uses them to decide which games to show to millions of players.

(Steam is the main online store for PC games, like the App Store but for games.)

Steam Next Fest is a huge week‑long showcase of upcoming games, and games that go in with strong wishlist numbers get featured the most.

We’re aiming for 10,000 wishlists by June. We can’t get there without you.

The story behind it

Why we made this game

We believe the media we consume is flooded with dystopias on purpose, to make it harder to imagine a different way of organizing ourselves. To shrink the horizon. To make the current order feel inevitable. We think, right now, one of the most needed things in our world is imagination rooted in hope.

In the world we imagined, the current concentrations of power fell, and what replaced them wasn’t a new empire. It was something messier and more hopeful: communes and tribes shaped by the biomes they call home, bound by ecosystems and mutual responsibility rather than capital and walls.

Old tensions don’t disappear, of course. So the factions built the Rites of Accord — ritualized contests fought in digital arenas before a watching world. Skill and strategy settle disputes, not bombs.

For us, this game is a way of practicing hope. Hope grounded in systems, in community, in the genuine belief that rebuilding is possible. We’re tired of futures that only imagine ruin. We wanted to make something that dares to imagine otherwise.
